Every match since the first kick-off in October 2014: how the league has changed, who won it, and who the best players at each position have been, measured with per-match stats and expected goals (xG).

1 · The league

Goals have settled near three a game, but chances are harder to finish

Scoring jumped after the first season and has hovered between 2.4 and 3.1 goals a match since. Teams take about the same number of shots as they did in 2019–20, but fewer of them go in.

The season without home advantage

In 2020–21 the whole league was played behind closed doors in Goa, so no team was really at home. Home wins fell to 30% and draws rose to 39%. Home advantage came back as soon as fans and home grounds did.

4 · Crowds

Crowds have more than halved since the early seasons

The ISL drew about 27,000 a match in 2015. By 2025–26 the average was 8,500. The 2020–21 and 2021–22 seasons were played behind closed doors. Kolkata's two giants, Mohun Bagan and East Bengal, draw by far the biggest crowds at the Salt Lake Stadium. All ten of the largest recent attendances were there, topped by 62,542 for the Kolkata derby in 2022–23.

5 · Best XI

The best XI since 2019–20

The top-rated player at each spot of a 4-3-3, from players with at least 2,000 minutes since 2019–20, when player stats begin. The number on each player is their position score out of 100: how they rank against everyone else who played that position. The Indian XI applies the same scores to Indian players only.

Scores compare players within the same position, so a 90 for a keeper and a 90 for a striker mean the same thing: better than about 90% of qualified players at that position.

6 · By position

    8 · Method and data

    Where the numbers come from

    Data. Match, player and shot data comes from FotMob. Results and goals cover every season from 2014. Per-player match stats start in 2019–20 (2018–19 has them for only 15 matches), and xG and shot maps start in 2020–21. StatsBomb also publishes free event data for 2021–22.

    Crowds. Match attendances come from FotMob for 2019–20 and from 2022–23 onward. For 2014 to 2018–19 the page uses the published league averages from Wikipedia, which match FotMob exactly for the seasons both cover. Clubs and stadiums only appear from FotMob seasons.

    Positions. Each player's position is where they most often started: goalkeeper, centre-back, full-back or wing-back, central midfielder, attacking midfielder or winger, or striker.

    Rankings. Stats are converted to per-90-minute rates and percentages. All-time rankings need 2,000 minutes and season rankings need 900. Each stat becomes a percentile among qualified players at the same position, and the percentiles are combined with position-specific weights, shown under each position table above.

    Limits. Event data can't capture everything: positioning, pressing and leadership don't show up in tackles and passes. A player's score also depends on the team around them. Treat the rankings as a well-informed starting point for argument, not a final verdict.
